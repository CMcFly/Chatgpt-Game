# EMBERWILD — GAME OVERVIEW

> **Status:** Living design document  
> **Purpose:** Define what Emberwild is supposed to become, independent of temporary prototype limitations or implementation shortcuts.

---

# 1. HIGH-LEVEL CONCEPT

**Emberwild** is a progression-heavy fantasy RPG designed around exploration, character growth, gathering, crafting, combat, rebuilding settlements, discovering a large world, and eventually participating in a shared multiplayer environment.

The game begins as a highly controlled personal story.

The player awakens in an unfamiliar fantasy world with a LitRPG-style **System** integrated into their perception. They do not understand the world, the System, or why they were brought there.

The early game slowly expands outward:

**Survive → learn → reach Hearthcross → help rebuild it → grow stronger → repair the route out → enter the wider world.**

The long-term experience should feel like a mixture of:

- open-world RPG exploration
- MMO-style progression
- RuneScape-like skill development
- LitRPG/System progression
- settlement restoration
- gathering and crafting
- dungeons
- hunting rare materials and creatures
- player housing/property
- long-term character advancement

Emberwild should reward curiosity rather than making the player feel like they are following a checklist forever.

---

# 2. CORE PLAYER FANTASY

The player begins as almost nothing.

They are:

- in an unknown world
- physically weak
- classless / **Unbound**
- unfamiliar with local creatures
- unfamiliar with local geography
- dependent on scraps and help from others

Over time, they become someone capable of changing the world around them.

The progression fantasy should eventually become:

> I arrived here with nothing.
>
> I learned how this world works.
>
> I became powerful.
>
> I built a home.
>
> I helped settlements grow.
>
> I explored places most people never reach.
>
> I defeated creatures that once would have annihilated me.
>
> I left permanent marks on the world.

The game should continually create visible evidence of that growth.

---

# 3. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

The primary loop is:

**Explore → discover resources/threats/quests → fight or gather → earn materials/XP/knowledge → improve character/gear/home/settlement → unlock new areas and systems → explore farther.**

Different players should be able to lean heavily into different portions of that loop.

A combat-focused player may spend their time:

- farming monsters
- completing combat quests
- clearing dungeons
- fighting bosses
- hunting rare drops

A gathering-focused player may spend their time:

- logging
- mining
- fishing
- hunting
- collecting rare materials
- supplying crafting/economy systems

An exploration-focused player may spend their time:

- discovering hidden locations
- finding unusual NPCs
- locating rare creatures
- uncovering ruins
- discovering world lore
- unlocking shortcuts and Waystones

A property-focused player may spend their time:

- gathering building materials
- expanding their home
- crafting furniture
- building workshops
- creating storage systems
- decorating
- hosting friends

These activities should feed into one another rather than exist as isolated minigames.

---

# 4. THE SYSTEM

The player experiences a LitRPG-style interface referred to simply as the **System**.

The System handles things such as:

- player status
- level
- Path/class
- health
- mana
- experience
- quests
- Journal entries
- discoveries
- maps
- creature analysis
- warnings
- skills
- stat progression
- notifications

The System should feel different from the physical world.

The world is fantasy.

The System feels precise, artificial, and somewhat mysterious.

Example:

> **SYSTEM WARNING**  
> Hostile entity detected.

or:

> **ENTITY ANALYSIS FAILED**  
> Current recommendation: RUN.

The exact origin of the System is intentionally not fully defined yet.

It may eventually become a major part of the central story.

---

# 5. CHARACTER PROGRESSION

Characters have traditional RPG progression alongside broader skill progression.

## Levels

Combat and general gameplay award experience.

Leveling increases character strength and unlocks new abilities/content.

Each level currently follows the design principle:

- automatic growth determined by the player's Path
- one free stat point for player-directed growth

The exact numerical balance is not final.

## Core Stats

Current core concepts include:

- HP
- MP
- ATK
- DEF
- MAG
- SPD

Additional stats may be introduced only when they add meaningful gameplay.

Avoid unnecessary stat bloat.

---

# 6. PATHS / CLASSES

The player begins the game as:

**UNBOUND**

They do not choose a class immediately upon spawning.

The initial tutorial should establish that the player currently lacks a stable Path.

After surviving Hollowpath and contributing to Hearthcross, the System stabilizes enough for the first Path selection.

Current starter Paths:

## Vanguard

Durable frontline combatant.

Natural progression favors:

**DEF**

Expected playstyle:

- durability
- direct fighting
- defensive skills
- survivability

## Arcanist

Magic-focused combatant.

Natural progression favors:

**MAG**

Expected playstyle:

- spellcasting
- elemental or arcane attacks
- mana management
- magical utility

## Shade

Fast opportunistic combatant.

Natural progression favors:

**SPD**

Expected playstyle:

- speed
- critical attacks
- evasion
- exploiting weakened enemies

Players should eventually be able to unlock additional Paths.

Switching between already unlocked Paths should not require permanently recreating the character.

Long-term progression may include:

- advanced Paths
- hybrid builds
- class quests
- Path-specific abilities
- rare specializations

Exact systems remain open for future design.

---

# 7. SKILLS AND PROFESSIONS

Emberwild should eventually contain a broad skill system inspired by games where non-combat progression matters just as much as combat.

Likely major categories include:

- Combat
- Woodcutting / Forestry
- Mining
- Fishing
- Hunting
- Crafting
- Cooking
- gathering-related professions
- potentially Merchant/Courier-style skills
- other specialized professions as the world expands

Skills should unlock:

- better tools
- new materials
- new locations
- crafting recipes
- quests
- special interactions
- economic opportunities

Higher progression should allow the player to interact with the world differently rather than simply increasing numbers.

---

# 8. COMBAT

Combat currently uses a turn-based/modal prototype.

The exact final combat presentation is still flexible, but several design principles are established.

## Creature Behaviors

Creatures are not all automatically hostile.

They can be:

### Passive
Avoid the player or flee.

### Neutral
Ignore the player unless attacked or otherwise provoked.

### Aggressive
Attack when the player enters their threat range.

This distinction should make the world feel more believable.

## Aggro and Leashing

Aggressive creatures should have:

- defined aggro distance
- pursuit behavior
- leash range
- return behavior

Large or powerful creatures should not chase players across half the region.

Bosses can have deliberately small aggro zones when appropriate.

## Old Enemies Becoming Weak

It is acceptable—and desirable—for early enemies to eventually become trivial.

Progress should feel powerful.

A monster that terrified the player at Level 2 should not remain equally threatening at Level 50 merely because the game scales everything upward.

## Future Combat Convenience

Possible later systems include:

- auto-fight
- automatic use of healing items
- configurable healing thresholds
- cooldown management
- combat presets

These should reduce repetitive input without removing meaningful tactical decisions.

---

# 9. LOOT AND EQUIPMENT

Enemies can drop:

- gold
- equipment
- monster-specific materials
- rare crafting components
- special drops

## Equipment Rarity

Current rarity ladder:

- Common
- Fine
- Rare
- Epic
- Legendary

Starter content should mostly produce:

- Common
- Fine
- Rare

Early bosses may rarely produce Epic items.

Legendary equipment should remain genuinely special.

## Monster Materials

Creatures should have distinct crafting materials.

Examples from current prototypes include:

- Moss Gel
- Ash Rat Hide
- Thorn Fiber
- Wisp Dust
- Stoneback Plate
- Stag Hide
- Briar Heart
- Ancient Fragment

These materials should eventually support:

- crafting
- upgrades
- quests
- settlement improvements
- economy/trading

## Equipment Comparison

Inventory interfaces should clearly show whether an item improves or reduces current stats.

Players should not need a calculator to determine whether a sword is better.

---

# 10. EXPLORATION

Exploration is one of Emberwild's most important systems.

The game should consistently reward players who leave the obvious path.

Possible discoveries include:

- hidden chests
- unusual gathering nodes
- rare creatures
- hidden NPCs
- abandoned camps
- caves
- ruins
- lore
- secret quests
- shortcuts
- Waystones
- rare resources
- dungeon entrances
- settlement locations

The world should encourage:

> “What is over there?”

rather than:

> “Which quest marker do I follow next?”

---

# 11. QUEST DESIGN

Quests can come from many sources.

Not everything needs to start with an NPC standing under an exclamation mark.

Potential quest sources:

- NPC conversations
- notice boards
- contracts
- discovered objects
- unusual item drops
- environmental discoveries
- overheard conversations
- unusual monster kills
- hidden NPCs
- dungeon discoveries
- settlement needs
- world events
- profession progression
- class/Path quests

Tutorial quests should thoroughly teach mechanics.

Once the core mechanics are understood, the game should stop treating the player like they need instructions every thirty seconds.

---

# 12. WORLD STRUCTURE

Emberwild should consist of large connected regions.

A single region can be large enough that walking directly across it may take roughly:

**5–10 minutes**

depending on terrain and movement progression.

Regions may be separated by natural or designed boundaries such as:

- mountain passes
- rivers
- gates
- tunnels
- ferries
- story boundaries
- Waystones
- loading transitions

This allows regions to feel genuinely large without requiring the entire game to exist on one enormous continuous map.

---

# 13. STARTER REGION — MOSSROAD VALE

The current starter region is **Mossroad Vale**.

It serves several purposes:

- introduce the world
- teach basic systems
- establish Hearthcross
- allow early exploration
- introduce gathering
- introduce dungeons
- introduce stronger monsters
- establish settlement restoration
- eventually prepare the player to enter the wider world

Known or currently planned areas include:

- Hearthcross
- Wayfarer Camp
- Old Mossroad Ruins
- Ironroot Quarry
- Glasswater Pool
- Briar Grove
- Barrowroot Ruins
- surrounding forests
- trails and resource areas

These locations and exact geography remain subject to redesign while the world is still being prototyped.

---

# 14. OPENING — HOLLOWPATH

The game begins before the player reaches Mossroad Vale proper.

## Isekai Introduction

The player remembers another world.

Something happened.

They awaken in Emberwild.

The System activates but has limited functionality.

Initial identity:

- Name: player-selected
- Class/Path: Unbound
- Location: Unknown

## Hollowpath

Hollowpath is a private tutorial area.

It should feel like a genuine forest location rather than a tutorial hallway.

The intended progression is:

**Wake → investigate surroundings → find supplies → learn inventory/equipment → explore branching paths → encounter passive wildlife → discover signs of a dangerous predator → fight basic enemies → discover signs of Hearthcross → increasingly ominous warnings → Veilfang attack.**

The tutorial should teach mechanics through meaningful situations rather than arbitrary instructions.

## Veilfang

Veilfang is the player's first encounter with something completely beyond their ability.

The encounter should be heavily foreshadowed.

Signs include:

- mauled animals
- extreme claw marks
- fleeing wildlife
- distant movement
- growls
- failed System scans

When Veilfang finally appears, the System should immediately communicate that something is wrong.

The player should initially attempt to escape.

Eventually they are forced into combat.

Their attacks barely affect the creature.

The System continually recalculates the danger.

Example:

> Threat analysis: FAILED.
>
> Estimated level difference: EXTREME.
>
> Survival probability: 0.7%.

The player loses.

This is a narrative defeat, not a normal game-over screen.

The player blacks out.

---

# 15. HEARTHCROSS

Rhea rescues the player and brings them to **Hearthcross**.

Hearthcross is not meant to begin as a thriving town.

It is a damaged frontier foothold.

Depending on final world lore, it may belong to a larger kingdom or political region.

At the beginning it is:

- damaged
- undermanned
- under-supplied
- partially abandoned
- vulnerable to surrounding wilderness

This creates space for the player to visibly transform it.

## Rhea

Rhea is the player's initial connection to Hearthcross.

She saved the player's life.

She also expects repayment.

The relationship begins roughly as:

> I saved your ass.
>
> You used supplies we desperately needed.
>
> You owe me.

The player should not remain simply because Rhea refuses to let them leave.

Their motivations overlap:

- they owe Rhea
- they do not understand this world
- Hearthcross provides relative safety
- the surrounding region is dangerous
- helping Hearthcross also helps the player

Over time the emotional progression should change from:

**forced obligation**

into:

**genuine investment in Hearthcross.**

Eventually the player may technically repay the debt but choose to continue helping anyway.

---

# 16. HEARTHCROSS RESTORATION

One of the earliest major progression systems is rebuilding Hearthcross.

Tutorial and early quests introduce game mechanics by solving real settlement problems.

Examples:

## Timber

Hearthcross needs lumber.

This introduces:

- woodcutting
- gathering
- material storage

The lumber visibly repairs something.

## Stone / Ore

The settlement needs metal or stone.

This introduces:

- mining
- quarrying
- crafting

The materials may restore:

- forge
- walls
- tools
- bridge components

## Food

The settlement needs reliable food.

This introduces:

- fishing
- hunting
- cooking
- gathering

## Threats

Creatures threaten the area.

This introduces:

- combat
- hunting
- monster materials
- road safety

## Salvage

Ruins contain useful material.

This introduces:

- exploration
- dungeons
- ancient materials
- hidden discoveries

## People

Stranded or hidden NPCs can be recruited or rescued.

Once brought to Hearthcross they may unlock:

- shops
- crafting
- new quests
- professions
- services

The key principle:

**The player should see Hearthcross change because of what they did.**

Buildings get repaired.

Fires light.

Walls improve.

NPCs arrive.

Services open.

The settlement becomes visibly healthier.

---

# 17. THE DESTROYED BRIDGE

The major exit from the starter region is blocked by a destroyed bridge across a dangerous river.

This is **not** intended to be a tiny tutorial objective.

Repairing the bridge is effectively a major starter-region progression goal.

Possible requirements include:

- stabilize Hearthcross
- establish timber production
- gather stone/ore
- repair the forge
- secure roads
- recruit necessary NPCs
- obtain specialized materials
- clear threats near the crossing
- craft bridge components
- complete regional story objectives

Repairing the bridge should feel like:

> We actually accomplished something.

Crossing it marks the player's transition into the wider game.

---

# 18. PRIVATE STARTER EXPERIENCE → SHARED WORLD

A strong future multiplayer structure is:

## Early Game

Hollowpath and early Hearthcross progression are personal/instanced.

This allows:

- personalized tutorial events
- settlement restoration
- story-specific NPC states
- controlled progression
- personal world changes

## Bridge Completion

Once the player has completed enough starter progression and repaired the route outward, they cross into the wider world.

At this point the game transitions toward:

**shared multiplayer / MMO-style spaces.**

Story-sensitive locations can remain instanced when necessary.

Examples:

- personal quest events
- class trials
- settlement attacks
- dungeons
- housing
- story-versioned locations

---

# 19. WAYSTONES

Waystones are the intended fast-travel system.

They are **not class-changing devices**.

A player discovers or activates Waystones throughout the world.

Once connected, they allow travel between known points.

Early travel should still encourage exploration.

Fast travel becomes convenience after locations have already been discovered.

---

# 20. DUNGEONS

Dungeons exist as separate instances entered through physical world entrances.

Preferred flow:

**discover entrance → interact → dungeon menu → solo or party selection → enter instance → complete dungeon → return to entrance.**

Current prototype:

**Barrowroot Ruins**

Long-term dungeon features may include:

- bosses
- difficulty tiers
- rare loot
- dungeon-specific materials
- puzzles
- secret rooms
- quests
- party play
- repeatable content

Physical entrances should matter.

Dungeons should feel connected to the world rather than simply existing in a menu.

---

# 21. GATHERING

Resource gathering should feel tied to believable locations.

## Mining

Dedicated ore regions and mines should exist.

Different materials appear in different environments.

## Forestry

Different regions and tree types can produce different woods/materials.

## Fishing

Fishing should generally depend on:

- valid water
- bait
- biome
- fishing skill
- equipment
- possible weather/time conditions

Avoid forcing fishing into tiny glowing interaction nodes everywhere.

## Hunting

Wildlife and monster hunting can become a full progression path.

It may include:

- creature tracking
- rare variants
- trophies
- specialized materials
- traps
- hunting equipment

---

# 22. CRAFTING

Crafting converts gathered and monster materials into useful progression.

Potential categories include:

- weapons
- armor
- tools
- consumables
- building components
- furniture
- settlement upgrades
- profession equipment

Crafting should be useful throughout the game rather than becoming obsolete immediately when loot starts dropping.

---

# 23. PLAYER HOUSING

Player housing is a major planned system.

The player can eventually earn, find, or purchase a homesite.

Housing is preferably instanced.

A physical location in the shared world acts as the entrance.

Inside, the player has considerable freedom.

Possible progression:

**Room → House → Homestead → Estate**

Potential features:

- walls
- floors
- furniture
- decoration
- storage
- crafting stations
- gardens
- trophies
- utility rooms
- guest permissions
- friend visits

The technical foundation should eventually be capable of scaling toward:

- Guild Halls
- larger properties
- settlement-scale construction

Full player-owned cities are **not currently established as guaranteed canon**.

---

# 24. STORAGE

Inventory management should exist.

Inventory frustration should not be the game.

The long-term target is a property storage network.

Instead of manually moving crafting materials between fifteen different chests, crafting stations can pull required materials automatically.

Possible priority:

**Player Inventory → Local Storage → Shared Property Storage**

Desired features include:

- deposit all materials
- material categories
- crafting from storage
- building from storage
- remote storage management
- storage upgrades
- specialized storage

The principle:

> Storage management can be satisfying.
>
> Storage inconvenience should not be gameplay.

---

# 25. ECONOMY

The game will eventually include meaningful economic systems.

Potential systems include:

- NPC shops
- player trading
- auction house
- Bazaar-style commodity market
- crafting economy
- resource trading
- service professions

Economy should not be seriously balanced until major gold/resource sinks exist.

Trying to perfectly tune money generation during the prototype stage is intentionally avoided.

---

# 26. DEATH

Normal combat death should clearly feel like death.

Current direction:

**YOU DIED**

followed by:

**Respawn at Hearthcross**

Possible penalties can include:

- portion of carried gold
- temporary inconvenience
- location reset

Death penalties should matter without becoming so harsh that players avoid exploration.

Narrative defeats such as Veilfang are separate from normal death mechanics.

---

# 27. UI / SYSTEM INTERFACE

The game interface should reinforce the LitRPG concept.

The world itself is fantasy.

The System UI should feel more artificial and precise.

Current visual direction:

- dark translucent panels
- charcoal / forest tones
- restrained green
- gold/amber highlights
- clean System-style typography
- subtle glow rather than excessive sci-fi neon

Main HUD concepts:

## Top Left

Player status:

- name
- Path
- level
- HP
- MP
- XP / relevant stats

## Top Right

Minimap.

## Objective Tracker

Compact tracked Journal entry.

## Bottom Dock

Current planned shortcuts:

- Inventory — I
- Character — C
- Journal — J
- Map — M
- System/Pause Menu — ESC

Only systems that actually exist should appear in the dock.

Do not clutter the interface with disabled promises of future features.

---

# 28. JOURNAL

The old **Journey** terminology has been replaced by:

**Journal**

Possible long-term sections:

- Active Quests
- Completed Quests
- Discoveries
- Bestiary
- Characters
- Lore
- Tutorials
- System entries
- Collections

The framing is that the System automatically records relevant information.

---

# 29. MAPS

The minimap should **not** use fog-of-war.

It is primarily a navigation aid.

The larger world map may use discovery-based fog or incomplete information.

Maps can show:

- terrain
- roads
- water
- known landmarks
- player position
- tracked objective
- discovered Waystones
- important locations

Unknown areas should retain mystery.

---

# 30. UI CUSTOMIZATION / DEVELOPER TOOLS

During development, Emberwild includes a HUD editor.

Developer Mode allows HUD pieces to be repositioned.

Current editable elements include concepts such as:

- status panel
- objective tracker
- minimap
- notifications
- interaction prompt
- bottom dock

This is primarily a development tool but could inform eventual player UI customization.

---

# 31. VISUAL DIRECTION

The current primitive graphics are temporary.

Emberwild is intended to move toward actual sprites/textures for:

- player
- NPCs
- mobs
- buildings
- trees
- props
- terrain

The technical direction should support swapping individual graphical assets without rewriting gameplay code.

The existing prototype renderer should remain available as fallback while assets are replaced gradually.

Preferred replacement order:

1. Player
2. Primary NPCs
3. Common early mobs
4. Trees/environment
5. Hearthcross buildings
6. Additional mobs/props
7. terrain/tile refinement

Avoid replacing the entire rendering system at once.

---

# 32. MULTIPLAYER

Multiplayer is a long-term goal.

It should not dictate every early prototype decision.

The priority is:

> Make the single-player gameplay loop fun first.

Then multiplayer can expand it.

Potential multiplayer systems include:

- shared regions
- parties
- dungeons
- trading
- settlements
- player economy
- guilds
- social spaces
- visiting player homes
- world events

Backend/multiplayer complexity should come after core gameplay proves itself.

# 35. DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## Progress Must Feel Visible

The player should see what changed.

Better gear should look and feel better.

Hearthcross should visibly improve.

New abilities should change gameplay.

New regions should feel meaningfully more dangerous or exotic.

## Exploration Matters

The obvious road should not contain everything valuable.

## Early Danger Should Become Later Power Fantasy

Creatures that once terrified the player can eventually become farming targets.

## Systems Should Interconnect

Mining exists because materials matter.

Crafting exists because crafted items matter.

Settlements need those items.

Economy values those resources.

Exploration reveals better resource locations.

Nothing should feel like an isolated mobile minigame bolted onto the RPG.

## Teach Thoroughly, Then Get Out of the Player's Way

The tutorial should explain systems clearly.

Afterward, let the player play.

## Avoid Premature Balance

Prototype values exist to make systems testable.

They are not sacred.

## Convenience Is Not the Enemy

Remove unnecessary friction.

Examples:

- crafting from storage
- Waystone travel
- clear equipment comparisons
- configurable combat automation later

Time should be spent making decisions and exploring—not fighting menus.

---

# 36. DEVELOPMENT PRIORITY

The broad development order remains:

## Phase A — Starter Region

Make Mossroad Vale and Hearthcross genuinely enjoyable to explore.

## Phase B — RPG Foundation

Strengthen:

- levels
- stats
- Paths
- loot
- skills
- crafting
- gathering

## Phase C — Dungeons

Develop proper repeatable dungeon content.

## Phase D — Additional Regions

Expand the world.

## Phase E — Long-Term Progression

Collections, advanced professions, higher-level systems.

## Phase F — Convenience / Meta Systems

Travel improvements, automation, property systems, deeper UI.

## Phase G — Multiplayer Backend

Only after the underlying game is already fun.

# 38. WHAT EMBERWILD SHOULD FEEL LIKE

The goal is not simply:

> a browser RPG.

It should feel like:

> being dropped into a huge fantasy world where almost everything starts unknown, then slowly becoming someone who understands it, survives it, exploits it, improves it, builds within it, and eventually becomes powerful enough to shape it.

The player should regularly have moments of:

> “What the hell is that?”

followed hours later by:

> “Oh, I know exactly what that is now.”

and eventually:

> “I used to be scared of those things.”

That progression—from confusion to mastery—is the heart of Emberwild.
