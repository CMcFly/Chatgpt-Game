# Emberwild — Early-Game Questline Research
## Effective Onboarding, System Teaching, Exploration, and Settlement Restoration

## Research Goal

This research examines quest and onboarding patterns that fit Emberwild's intended genre:

- progression-heavy fantasy RPG
- open-world exploration
- MMO-style character progression
- gathering and crafting
- settlement restoration
- long-term housing/building systems
- dungeons and regional progression
- a guided early game that opens into player freedom

The goal is not to copy another game's questline. It is to identify proven design patterns that can guide the rewrite of Hollowpath, Hearthcross, and Mossroad Vale.

---

## 1. Core Finding: Teach Only What the Player Needs Now

Strong onboarding avoids front-loading every system. Hollowpath should teach the minimum survival vocabulary needed to understand the game:

- move
- interact
- inspect
- gather something
- craft something simple
- equip something
- fight
- heal
- explore
- understand threat
- run

It should not attempt to fully teach logging, mining, fishing, hunting, cooking, advanced crafting, settlement systems, professions, shops, Paths, and dungeons before Hearthcross.

**Hollowpath = mechanical literacy.**

**Hearthcross = systemic literacy.**

---

## 2. Teach Through Immediate Purpose

Traditional MMO checklist onboarding often teaches controls without explaining why systems matter.

For Emberwild, every Hearthcross teaching quest should answer:

**Why does Hearthcross need this?**

Bad:
> Gather 10 logs to learn Woodcutting.

Better:
> The remaining shelter cannot survive another storm. Find usable timber so the carpenter can brace it.

Bad:
> Catch 5 fish to learn Fishing.

Better:
> Food stores are almost empty. The river is currently the fastest reliable source of protein.

Bad:
> Mine 8 copper ore to learn Mining.

Better:
> The settlement recovered tools, but several are broken. The smith can repair them if you can find workable ore.

Tutorial actions should create narrative consequences.

---

## 3. Visible Consequence Is a Major Hook

Settlement-restoration games such as Dragon Quest Builders / Builders 2 demonstrate the value of tying gathering, crafting, exploration, NPC requests, and community growth together.

Hearthcross should visibly change after meaningful quest chapters:

- empty food shelves become stocked
- sick NPCs recover
- timber piles appear
- ruined foundations gain scaffolding
- forge smoke begins
- palisade gaps disappear
- paths get cleared
- lanterns appear
- population increases
- ambient dialogue changes
- workers begin performing tasks

The strongest reward can be:

> I did that.

---

## 4. NPC Contribution Should Rise as the Town Recovers

Early on, the player does most work because Hearthcross is helpless.

As the settlement improves:

- Stage 0: player does nearly everything
- Stage 2: NPCs process supplied resources
- Stage 4: specialists construct buildings after resources are delivered
- Stage 6: Hearthcross passively repairs minor damage and produces common resources
- Bridge phase: player solves difficult bottlenecks while the recovered town supplies labor

This demonstrates that Hearthcross itself has recovered and foreshadows later player settlements.

---

## 5. Multiple Valid Contributions Beat Rigid Checklists

Guild Wars 2-style area objectives suggest a useful model: represent some restoration goals as **needs** rather than exact item checklists.

Example:

### Hearthcross Food Supply: 0 / 100

Possible contributions:

- fish
- hunted meat
- edible plants
- mushrooms
- cooked meals
- later farm produce

The first fishing quest can still explicitly teach fishing, but later the game should say **Hearthcross needs food**, not **catch exactly five trout because tutorial**.

This gives players autonomy and supports different play preferences.

---

## 6. Systems Should Interlock

Avoid completely isolated Fishing Quest → Logging Quest → Mining Quest → Crafting Quest chains.

Instead:

### Repair the Workshop
- timber from logging
- stone from mining
- bindings from crafting

### Restore the Infirmary
- medicinal herbs
- cloth/fiber
- perhaps monster material

### Stabilize Food Stores
- fishing
- gathering
- hunting
- cooking

### Restore the Forge
- ore
- charcoal from wood
- recovered tools
- later specialized materials

The player should learn a network, not a menu of unrelated minigames.

---

## 7. Hearthcross Should Preview Housing and Building

Palia is a useful onboarding case study because its developers revised the early game to get players into housing and skill guidance sooner while preserving freedom to explore.

Emberwild does not need to award a full personal house immediately, but Hearthcross should teach that:

- structures can be repaired
- structures have functional purposes
- resources create permanent upgrades
- spaces change because of player decisions

Later player housing then feels like a natural expansion of something the player already understands.

---

## 8. Gradually Release the Player

Recommended guidance curve:

### Very Guided — Hollowpath
Player is vulnerable and needs explicit direction.

### Guided Hub — Early Hearthcross
Several needs exist, but recommendations are clear.

### Guided Choice — Mid Hearthcross
Multiple restoration projects become active at once.

Example:
- stabilize food
- repair workshop
- secure east road

Player chooses order.

### Regional Freedom — Late Hearthcross / Mossroad Vale
Objectives point toward problems and destinations instead of prescribing exact actions.

### Open Region
Quest markers become aids rather than constant instructions.

---

## 9. The Bridge Is the Starter-Region Final Exam

Bridge repair is a strong multi-system capstone.

It can require:

- established food supply
- timber infrastructure
- metalworking
- repaired tools
- safe roads
- recruited specialists
- regional exploration
- a dungeon material
- resolution of a major local threat

Nothing about the bridge should be a brand-new concept. It combines what the player already learned at meaningful scale.

---

## 10. Quests Need Emotional Meaning

Hearthcross cannot be only a progression machine. The player needs people to care about.

Possible human stories:

- a parent giving their portion of food to a child
- someone refusing medicine because supplies are scarce
- a carpenter unable to work because their tools were lost
- a fisher whose boat was destroyed
- residents arguing whether Hearthcross should be abandoned
- someone who left for supplies and never returned
- a blacksmith refusing to return until the road is safe
- a villager initially hostile to the outsider gradually becoming an ally

"10 fish" means something different if those fish are dinner for people the player knows.

---

## 11. Autonomy, Mastery, Relatedness

Useful motivational goals:

### Autonomy
- choose which Hearthcross need to address next
- choose resource contributions
- choose exploration routes
- optional discoveries
- later construction decisions

### Mastery
- primitive spear → real equipment
- basic tools → upgraded tools
- dangerous early areas → manageable later
- crude recipes → specialized crafting

### Relatedness
Build relationships with:
- Rhea
- surviving villagers
- recruited specialists
- people rescued during exploration

Hearthcross uniquely supports all three.

---

## 12. Quest Density Should Be Layered, Not Linear

The existing prototype effectively moves through:

1. kill Mosslings
2. go to Wayfarer Camp
3. find ruins
4. free roam

The stronger model layers several quest types:

- Main Restoration Thread
- Character Threads
- Profession Threads
- Exploration/Discovery Threads
- Threat Threads
- Dungeon Thread

A single trip can advance several layers at once.

---

## 13. Suggested Early-Game Shape

### Phase A — Hollowpath
- System boot
- inspect surroundings
- salvage scraps
- gather primitive materials
- craft wooden spear
- optional herb/healing discovery
- first weak enemy
- signs of powerful predator
- exploration choice
- Hearthcross clue
- Veilfang chase
- scripted defeat/rescue

### Phase B — Hearthcross Survival
- food
- medicine
- emergency shelter
- foraging/fishing/basic cooking
- town visibly changes

### Phase C — Hearthcross Infrastructure
- logging
- mining
- crafting
- tools
- processing
- workshop/storage/forge

### Phase D — Hearthcross Security
- aggro
- hunting
- monster materials
- road safety
- palisade/patrols

### Phase E — Hearthcross Community
- NPC recruitment
- Wayfarer Camp
- specialist quests
- shops/professions/services

### Phase F — Mossroad Recovery
- Old Mossroad Ruins
- Ironroot Quarry
- Glasswater Pool
- Briar Grove
- Barrowroot Ruins

### Phase G — Bridge Project
- strategic bottlenecks solved
- NPCs perform much of the physical construction
- bridge visibly progresses
- crossing ends extended onboarding

---

## 14. Avoid the Tutorial Prison

Settlement restoration can remain engaging only if players get agency.

Safeguards:

- optional side paths begin early
- resources can be discovered before quests request them
- multiple restoration needs can be active concurrently
- profession depth remains optional
- Mossroad Vale exploration opens before full restoration
- bridge gates the wider world, not every starter-region activity
- advanced tutorials appear contextually

The game should gradually stop saying:

> Do this.

and start saying:

> Here's the problem.

---

## 15. Research-Based Design Rules

1. Introduce mechanics when useful.
2. Do not introduce systems merely because they exist.
3. Every tutorial objective needs an in-world reason.
4. Prefer local problems over arbitrary kill/gather counts.
5. Allow multiple valid contributions where practical.
6. Make systems feed one another.
7. Reward with visible world changes as well as XP/items.
8. Let NPC contribution increase as the settlement recovers.
9. Use Hearthcross to preview housing/town construction.
10. Shift gradually from instructions to player-directed problem solving.
11. Let exploration discover quests.
12. Make NPC relationships part of motivation.
13. Use bridge repair as a multi-system capstone.
14. Require literacy, not mastery, in every profession.
15. Make restored Hearthcross dramatically different from arrival.
16. Revisit Hearthcross later so players can appreciate the change.
17. Playtest tutorial drop-off and confusion.
18. Preserve optional discoveries in guided sections.
19. Teach through gameplay before text when possible.
20. Treat Hearthcross as the prototype for Emberwild's future player-building fantasy.

## Particularly Relevant Reference Games

### Hero of Aethric
Useful for visual/world inspiration, map-based quest guidance, open-world RPG structure, home-base concepts, and class/loot progression. Avoid copying exact UI, world, characters, or quest structures.

### Dragon Quest Builders / Builders 2
Most relevant reference for Hearthcross: devastated settlements becoming healthy, gathering/building requests, villagers gaining capability, visible progression, communal projects, NPC recruitment, and building systems that lead naturally into player creativity.

### Palia
Useful for life-skill onboarding, housing as an early progression hook, skill interdependence, NPC mentors, and balancing clear guidance with exploration freedom.

### Guild Wars 2
Useful for area-based objectives, multiple valid actions, exploration-led discovery, dynamic events, and local problems that do not always require talking to a quest giver first.

### RuneScape
Useful for long-term noncombat skill value, quests that introduce systems/world lore, broad progression, and player-owned housing concepts.
