# Emberwild — Hearthcross Restoration & Early-Game Progression Plan

## Purpose

Hearthcross should be more than a starter village and more than a tutorial hub. It should be the player's first experience of **changing the world through gathering, crafting, exploration, combat, and community-building**.

The player arrives after surviving Hollowpath and being rescued by Rhea. Hearthcross is barely functioning: people are hungry, supplies are exhausted, structures are ruined, roads are unsafe, and the settlement does not have the resources or workforce needed to repair the destroyed Mossroad bridge.

The bridge is therefore not blocked because nobody knows how to repair it. It is blocked because Hearthcross is too broken to undertake a project that large.

The player must help the settlement survive, stabilize, rebuild, and grow before the town is capable of repairing the bridge.

This restoration arc should also act as foreshadowing and training for later player-driven building systems, including:

- player housing
- property upgrades
- workshops and production buildings
- settlement construction
- resource logistics
- possible player-founded or player-built towns
- long-term world-state changes

Hearthcross is the first controlled example of the broader fantasy:

> I arrived with nothing, learned how this world worked, and left a permanent mark on it.

---

## 1. Early-Game Teaching Structure

The opening sequence and Hearthcross should have different jobs.

### Hollowpath = Survival & Mechanical Basics

Hollowpath should teach enough for a new player to survive independently:

- movement
- interaction
- System prompts/scanning
- inventory
- gathering one or two simple resources
- primitive crafting
- equipping gear
- basic combat
- healing
- creature dispositions
- exploration and optional discoveries
- fleeing from enemies that are too powerful

Not every profession needs to appear here.

### First Weapon

Instead of simply finding a complete Crude Trail Knife and equipping it, the player should potentially craft their first real weapon.

Example:

1. Search an abandoned satchel/camp.
2. Recover useful scraps, cordage, or a damaged cutting tool.
3. Gather a sturdy branch.
4. The System unlocks a primitive recipe.
5. Craft a **Crude Wooden Spear**.

This introduces crafting organically and establishes the principle that found materials can be turned into useful equipment.

A knife or blade fragment can still exist as a tool or component without being handed to the player as a complete starter weapon.

### Early Healing

Silverleaf should not necessarily transform directly into a tonic. A simple healing recipe could instead teach basic resource combination:

- Silverleaf
- cloth/bandage material
- clean water or another basic component

Result: Field Poultice / Rough Restorative / similar primitive healing item.

The point is not to teach the entire crafting profession. It is to establish:

**Find resource → understand resource → craft useful item.**

---

## 2. Hearthcross = Systems Tutorial

Hearthcross should not repeat basic tutorials. It should teach how Emberwild's systems connect to progression, communities, economy, exploration, and permanent world change.

**Hollowpath teaches mechanics.**

**Hearthcross teaches systems.**

---

## 3. Hearthcross Arrival State

When the player first arrives, Hearthcross should barely qualify as a village. Ideally, only **one structure is truly usable**.

Possible initial presentation:

- one damaged communal shelter / Hearth House
- patched roof
- central fire
- bedrolls
- makeshift medical area
- nearly empty food storage
- damaged palisade
- collapsed buildings
- abandoned foundations
- broken carts and tools
- overgrown paths
- only a small number of exhausted NPCs

The player should understand the problem visually before being told everything through dialogue.

Hearthcross is hungry, undermanned, undersupplied, unsafe, partially abandoned, economically dead, and unable to spare labor or resources for the bridge.

---

## 4. Why the Bridge Cannot Be Repaired

The destroyed bridge is the major exit from Mossroad Vale, but it should not be treated as a simple resource gate.

When the player arrives, Hearthcross lacks:

- reliable food
- lumber production
- processed construction materials
- mining output
- forge capability
- replacement tools
- safe roads
- trained labor
- specialists
- spare resources
- political/economic stability

The bridge is effectively a luxury infrastructure project for a settlement focused on surviving the next few days.

The player does not merely gather materials for a bridge. The player builds the **economy, workforce, infrastructure, and security necessary for Hearthcross to build it**.

---

## 5. Proposed Hearthcross Restoration Arc

Avoid dozens of disconnected micro-tutorial quests. Use larger restoration chapters containing several connected objectives.

### Chapter 1 — Keep Them Alive

Primary problem: **The settlement is starving and running out of medicine.**

Systems deepened:

- foraging
- herbs
- fishing
- basic cooking
- hunting if appropriate
- resource quality/location
- gathering efficiency

Possible outcomes:

- food stores visibly fill
- communal cooking area improves
- injured NPCs recover
- herbalist/healing station becomes functional
- dialogue changes as desperation decreases

Lesson: gathering is not busywork. Resources keep people alive and change the world.

### Chapter 2 — Tools to Rebuild

Primary problem: **Hearthcross cannot rebuild without tools and construction materials.**

Systems deepened:

- logging
- mining
- resource tiers
- gathering tools
- material processing
- crafting
- storage/supply systems

Possible objectives:

- recover the lost tool crate
- gather usable timber
- collect stone
- mine basic ore
- produce charcoal or other processed resources
- restore a workshop
- repair/build the forge

Visible outcomes: lumber stacks, scaffolding, workshop opening, forge construction states, NPCs beginning to work.

### Chapter 3 — Make Hearthcross Safe

Primary problem: **The settlement cannot grow while creatures and road threats constantly destroy supplies and endanger workers.**

Systems deepened:

- combat
- creature aggression/dispositions
- hunting
- monster materials
- defensive structures
- patrols
- regional threat control

Visible outcomes: repaired walls, guards/patrols, safer roads, more NPC activity, larger settlement footprint.

### Chapter 4 — Bring People Back

Primary problem: **Hearthcross has resources and safety, but not enough skilled people.**

Systems deepened:

- exploration
- NPC recruitment
- specialist unlocks
- regional travel
- social quests
- settlement services

Potential specialists:

- blacksmith
- herbalist
- carpenter
- fisher
- merchant
- cook
- scout
- trainer
- builder

Possible destinations: Wayfarer Camp, abandoned homesteads, hidden camps, ruins, stranded traveler locations, other small settlements.

Core loop:

**Explore world → find people/resources → improve Hearthcross → Hearthcross gives new tools/services → explore farther.**

### Chapter 5 — Regional Recovery

Hearthcross now survives, but Mossroad Vale itself must become usable again.

Existing content can be folded into this phase:

- Wayfarer Camp
- Scout Mara
- Old Mossroad Ruins
- Ironroot Quarry
- Glasswater Pool
- Briar Grove
- Barrowroot Ruins

Each location should contribute to settlement recovery or the eventual bridge project.

Examples:

- quarry supplies stone/ore
- pool supports food/fishing progression
- ruins contain salvage or ancient components
- Briar Grove contains rare structural/crafting material
- Barrowroot introduces dungeon progression and specialized loot

---

## 6. Bridge Repair as the Starter-Region Capstone

Only after Hearthcross is stable should the bridge become a realistic project.

Possible bridge requirements:

- food supply stabilized
- timber production established
- workshop operating
- forge restored
- mining/quarry access secured
- roads safer
- necessary specialists recruited
- regional threats reduced
- specialized materials recovered
- bridge construction components crafted

Bridge visual states:

1. destroyed crossing
2. cleared worksite
3. repaired foundations
4. scaffolding / temporary span
5. nearly complete bridge
6. completed Mossroad bridge

Bridge completion marks graduation from the extended early-game experience.

> Hearthcross no longer merely survives. It can build toward the future.

---

## 7. Settlement State & Persistence

Hearthcross should be driven by persistent settlement state rather than instantly appearing as a finished town after the prologue.

Possible conceptual stages:

- Stage 0 — Collapse
- Stage 1 — Survival
- Stage 2 — Basic supplies stabilized
- Stage 3 — Workshop / timber production
- Stage 4 — Forge / mining infrastructure
- Stage 5 — Food economy and profession services
- Stage 6 — Defenses and safer roads
- Stage 7 — Population / specialist recovery
- Stage 8 — Bridge construction
- Stage 9 — Restored Hearthcross

Player actions should permanently alter the settlement map.

Buildings should ideally have visible construction states:

- ruins/foundation
- supplies/scaffolding
- framed structure
- mostly complete
- completed building
- later upgrades

---

## 8. Buildings Should Be Functional Unlocks

Restoration should unlock gameplay, not just scenery.

- Herbalist / infirmary → healing, medicine, herb progression
- Carpenter / lumber yard → construction, woodworking
- Forge / blacksmith → equipment, mining progression, tool upgrades
- Fishery / dock → fishing progression and food economy
- Kitchen / tavern → cooking, quests, social hub
- Storehouse → settlement resource capacity and logistics
- Trail Guild → contracts, regional quests, scouting
- Path Trainer → Path/class progression
- Merchant → economy/trading
- Waystone → fast travel after regional conditions are met
- Bridge Works → final Mossroad Vale infrastructure project

---

## 9. Setup for Later Player Building

Hearthcross restoration should quietly teach concepts that become much larger later.

Early lesson: bring resources to a structure and improve it.

Later: build and upgrade your own house.

Further: add workshops, storage, production, decorations, and utility structures.

Potential endgame evolution: establish or help construct settlements/towns.

Systems introduced through Hearthcross can become foundations for:

- player housing
- buildable plots
- upgrade tiers
- construction recipes
- furniture/decorating
- resource storage
- production buildings
- NPC recruitment
- town specialization
- player-run services
- multiplayer settlement projects
- guild/community towns
- taxes/economy/logistics if desired

Hearthcross should feel like the player's first apprenticeship in changing settlements.

---

## 10. Design Principles

1. Teach through need, not pop-up tutorials.
2. Every major tutorial system should solve a real problem.
3. Avoid handing the player systems with no narrative reason.
4. Show visible changes after player effort.
5. Do not make every profession mandatory in Hollowpath.
6. Hearthcross deepens systems rather than introducing every button.
7. Quest objectives should overlap systems instead of isolating them.
8. NPCs should begin contributing as the settlement improves.
9. The player should not personally perform every repetitive construction action.
10. Bridge repair is the result of restoring Hearthcross, not merely collecting bridge materials.
11. The starter region should eventually stop tutoring and allow genuine exploration.
12. Hearthcross should become an emotional anchor the player wants to revisit later.
